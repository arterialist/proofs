## Summary
Peter Scholze's theory of perfectoid spaces establishes a profound tilting equivalence $(-)^\flat$ between characteristic $0$ and characteristic $p$ perfectoid fields ($K \to K^\flat = \lim_{x \mapsto x^p} K$), identifying étale sites and transforming Galois representations across characteristics. On the Fargues-Fontaine curve $X_{\mathrm{FF}}$, vector bundles geometrize local $p$-adic Hodge theory and the local Langlands correspondence.

However, the tilting functor and adic space geometry operate strictly locally at a single rational prime $p$. The geometric categories for distinct primes $p \ne \ell$ (such as $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ and $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$) are completely orthogonal and decoupled, possessing no unified geometric space or continuous global tensor product over $\mathbb{Z}$. Crucially, perfectoid geometry does not extend to the Archimedean place $\mathbb{R}$: there is no 'Archimedean tilting equivalence' or continuous Riemannian metric glue connecting the $p$-adic adic spaces to the vertical line $s = 1/2 + it$. Consequently, $p$-adic tilting cannot generate spectral coercivity over the continuous imaginary oscillations of $\zeta(s)$, leaving non-trivial zeros in the critical strip unconstrained.
