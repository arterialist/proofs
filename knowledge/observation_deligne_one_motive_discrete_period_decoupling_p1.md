## Summary
In mixed motives and Hodge theory (Deligne 1974, Barbieri-Viale, Srinivas), Deligne's 1-motives $M = [L 	o G]$ over a number field consist of a discrete lattice $L$ and a semi-abelian variety $G$ (an extension $0 	o T 	o G 	o A 	o 0$ of an abelian variety $A$ by an algebraic torus $T$). Deligne 1-motives represent the first non-trivial step in mixed Hodge structures, functorially unifying 0-cycles, 1-cycles, Cartier divisors, and generalized Picard and Albanese varieties. Under Hodge and de Rham realizations, period pairings evaluate integrals of algebraic forms along topological cycles, yielding algebraic logarithms and abelian integrals.

However, Deligne 1-motive period pairings evaluate discrete algebraic numbers and periods of algebraic varieties over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. They operate strictly within the algebraic-geometric category of mixed Hodge structures, which decouples completely from the continuous vertical transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of arithmetic Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, Deligne 1-motives provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
