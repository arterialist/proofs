## Summary
Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture and Odlyzko's extensive numerical computations demonstrate that the normalized zero spacings of the Riemann zeta function asymptotically conform to the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) random matrix statistics $1 - (\frac{\sin \pi u}{\pi u})^2$ with quadratic level repulsion $P(s) \sim \frac{32}{\pi^2} s^2$. However, GUE spectral rigidity is fundamentally a statistical bulk property of critical-line zeros. A finite or sub-linear collection of off-line zero pairs or quadruplets $\rho_0 = \beta_0 \pm i\gamma_0$ with $\beta_0 \ne 1/2$ has asymptotic density zero relative to the $N(T) \sim \frac{T}{2\pi} \log T$ bulk zeros, leaving the normalized 2-point pair correlation integral:
$$\lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{N(T)} \sum_{1 \le j \ne k \le N(T)} f\left( (\gamma_j - \gamma_k) \frac{\log T}{2\pi} \right)$$
completely invariant. Therefore, establishing GUE pair correlation or random unitary matrix eigenvalue statistics on the critical line provides zero logical leverage to exclude sparse off-line zeros and cannot serve as an unconditioned forward proof engine.
