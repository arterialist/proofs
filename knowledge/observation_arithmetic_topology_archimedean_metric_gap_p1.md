## Summary
Arithmetic topology, initiated by Mazur, Kapranov, and Reznikov, models the spectrum of the integers $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})$ as an arithmetic 3-manifold, where prime ideals $\mathfrak{p} = (p)$ correspond to embedded knots $S^1 \hookrightarrow M^3$ and étale fundamental groups $\pi_1(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}) \setminus \{p_1, \dots, p_k\})$ mirror knot complement groups. In this framework, arithmetic Reidemeister torsions and Alexander-Fox polynomials provide striking formal analogues of Iwasawa polynomials and $L$-functions at $s=0$ and $s=1$.

However, arithmetic topology is strictly topological and non-Archimedean. The fundamental analogies operate over profinite fundamental groups and étale cohomology, entirely lacking an Archimedean metric, continuous Riemannian structure, or Laplacian operator on $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})$. Consequently, there is no analytic continuation mechanism or unitary phase flow capable of generating the continuous complex variable $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ along the critical line $s = 1/2 + it$. Topological invariants such as linking numbers and Reidemeister torsion are invariant under complex phase rotations and provide zero analytical leverage to confine the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ to the critical line.
