## Summary
In motivic homotopy theory and algebraic cycle theory (Voevodsky, Rost, Suslin, Bloch, Kato), the norm residue isomorphism theorem (formerly the Bloch–Kato conjecture) establishes an isomorphism between Milnor $K$-theory mod $m$ and Galois cohomology $K_n^M(k)/m \cong H^n(k, \mu_m^{\otimes n})$. In Voevodsky's stable motivic homotopy category $\mathcal{SH}(k)$, the slice spectral sequence converges from motivic cohomology $H^{p,q}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ to algebraic cobordism $MGL$ and algebraic $K$-theory.

However, Bloch–Kato norm residue isomorphisms and motivic homotopy slice spectral sequences evaluate algebraic cycle groups graded by discrete integer pairs $(p, q) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$. They operate strictly in the discrete algebraic and Galois-cohomological category, which decouples completely from the continuous transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of arithmetic Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, motivic cohomology and slice spectral sequences provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
