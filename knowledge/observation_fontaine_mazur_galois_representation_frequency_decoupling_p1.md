## Summary
In arithmetic geometry and Galois representations (Fontaine, Mazur, Wiles, Taylor, Kisin), the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture characterizes continuous irreducible $p$-adic representations $\rho: \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ that are unramified outside a finite set of places and potentially semi-stable at $p$ as coming from the étale cohomology of algebraic varieties over number fields. The local structure at $p$ is governed by Fontaine's period rings ($B_{\mathrm{HT}}, B_{\mathrm{dR}}, B_{\mathrm{st}}, B_{\mathrm{cris}}$) and labeled by discrete integer Hodge–Tate weights.

However, Fontaine–Mazur geometric Galois representations evaluate discrete algebraic Frobenius eigenvalues $\alpha_{p,i} \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and integer Hodge–Tate weights $k_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. They operate in the algebraic and $p$-adic category, which decouples completely from the continuous transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of complex Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, Fontaine–Mazur potentially semi-stable Galois representations provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
