## Summary
In motivic Galois theory and transcendental numbers (Grothendieck, Deligne, André, Brown, Huber, Wüstholz), Grothendieck's period conjecture asserts that any polynomial algebraic relation with algebraic coefficients between the periods of an algebraic variety $X$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ arises from an algebraic cycle on some power $X^n$. The period isomorphism $\operatorname{comp}: H_{\mathrm{dR}}^n(X, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}) \otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} \mathbb{C} \cong H_{\mathrm{B}}^n(X(\mathbb{C}), \mathbb{Q}) \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{C}$ defines a torsor under the motivic Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}_{\mathrm{mot}}(X)$.

However, Grothendieck's period conjecture and motivic Galois torsors evaluate algebraic differential forms integrated along topological cycles $\int_\gamma \omega \in \mathbb{C}$. They operate strictly on the discrete period matrices and cycle relations of algebraic varieties over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, which decouple completely from the continuous transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, Grothendieck period torsors provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
