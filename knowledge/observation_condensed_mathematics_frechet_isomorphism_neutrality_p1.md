## Summary
In condensed mathematics (Clausen-Scholze), topological spaces are replaced by condensed sets—sheaves of sets on the site of pro-finite sets equipped with finite joint surjective covers. In condensed functional analysis, solid abelian groups $\mathbb{Z}^\blacksquare$ and liquid vector spaces $\mathbb{R}_{\le p}$ solve the classical failure of topological vector spaces to form an abelian category with well-behaved completed tensor products, enabling systematic derived categories of functional analysis over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$.

However, the fully faithful embedding of classical compactly generated Hausdorff spaces into condensed sets preserves the underlying topological and analytic structures. Specifically, when applied to complex analysis, the condensed vector space of entire functions $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})$ is naturally isomorphic in the condensed category to the classical nuclear Fréchet space of entire functions equipped with the compact-open topology. The zeros of an entire function $f(s)$ are intrinsic complex analytic invariants invariant under condensed reinterpretation: a point $\rho \in \mathbb{C}$ satisfies $f(\rho) = 0$ if and only if the evaluation morphism $\mathrm{ev}_\rho: \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{C}$ annihilates $f$. Condensed mathematics rectifies homological algebra and derived tensor products of functional analysis, but does not alter the values, functional equations, or root locations of $\zeta(s)$, leaving the distribution of zeros in the critical strip completely unconstrained.
