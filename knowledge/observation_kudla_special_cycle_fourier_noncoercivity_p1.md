## Summary
In arithmetic geometry and the Kudla program (Kudla, Rapoport, Yang), generating series of arithmetic special cycles $\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(q) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(n) q^n$ on orthogonal or unitary Shimura varieties $\mathrm{Sh}_K(G, X)$ are proven to be Fourier expansions of automorphic forms (such as Siegel or Hilbert modular forms). The arithmetic degrees and Gillet-Soulé heights $\langle \widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(n), \widehat{\omega}^{\dim - 1} \rangle$ equate to central derivatives of incoherent Eisenstein series: $\widehat{\operatorname{deg}}(\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(n)) = c \cdot E'(n, 1/2, \Phi)$.

However, Kudla's identity is an algebraic intersection relation operating at discrete Fourier modes $q^n = e^{2\pi i n \tau}$ and evaluating $L$-functions and their derivatives strictly at central symmetry points (e.g. $s = 1/2$ or $s = 1$). The generating series identity establishes modularity of arithmetic heights, but provides zero continuous vertical analytic continuation engine along $s = 1/2 + it$ for $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Most fundamentally, automorphic modularity alone is strictly insufficient to exclude off-line zeros (as demonstrated by Davenport-Heilbronn counterexamples), and discrete cycle intersection pairings generate zero transverse dynamical restoring force to prevent complex zero excursions of $\zeta(s)$ into the strip $1/2 < \operatorname{Re}(s) < 1$.
