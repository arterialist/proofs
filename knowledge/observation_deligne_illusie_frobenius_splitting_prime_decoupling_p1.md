## Summary
In algebraic geometry and characteristic $p$ Hodge theory (Deligne, Illusie), the liftability of a smooth scheme $X$ over $W_2(k)$ with $\dim X < p$ implies the decomposition of the de Rham complex $F_* \Omega^\bullet_{X/k} \simeq \bigoplus \Omega^i_{X'/k}[-i]$ in the derived category $D(X')$. This yields a purely algebraic proof of Hodge–de Rham degeneration and Kodaira vanishing without transcendental analysis.

However, Deligne–Illusie Cartier splitting operates strictly locally in characteristic $p$ for individual primes $p > \dim X$. The resulting algebraic isomorphisms decouple across distinct primes and lack Archimedean vertical transcendental continuity off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$. Characteristic $p$ Hodge degeneration provides zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$.
