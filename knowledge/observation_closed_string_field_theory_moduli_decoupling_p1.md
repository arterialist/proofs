## Summary
In non-perturbative string theory (Zwiebach), closed string field theory (CSFT) is defined on the Fock space of 2D conformal field theories with string vertices $V_{g, n}$ carving out fundamental domains of the Deligne–Mumford moduli space of stable curves $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g, n}$. The action $S(\Psi) = \frac{1}{2} \langle \Psi, c_0^- Q_B \Psi \rangle + \sum \frac{g^{2g-2+n}}{n!} \{ \Psi^n \}_g$ computes off-shell scattering amplitudes and tachyon condensation potentials via hyperbolic Riemann surface geometries.

However, CSFT string vertices compute geometric partition functions by integrating automorphic forms over the moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces $\mathcal{M}_g$. The resulting amplitudes inherit modular invariance under mapping class groups $\mathrm{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z})$, but automorphic modularity alone is strictly insufficient to exclude off-line zeros of associated $L$-functions (as proven by Davenport–Heilbronn counterexamples). The off-shell vertex boundaries and string field equations decouple from the microscopic distribution of non-trivial zeros along the vertical line $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
