## Summary
In symplectic topology and contact geometry, Symplectic Field Theory (SFT, Eliashberg-Givental-Hofer) associates to a contact manifold $(M, \alpha)$ a differential graded algebra whose generators are closed Reeb orbits $\gamma$ satisfying $\alpha(R_\alpha) = 1, d\alpha(R_\alpha, \cdot) = 0$. The differential counts $J$-holomorphic curves in the symplectization $(\mathbb{R} \times M, d(e^t \alpha))$ asymptotic to Reeb orbits at $\pm \infty$. In arithmetic models of contact flows, prime numbers are represented as closed Reeb orbits with contact action $T_p = \int_\gamma \alpha = \log p$.

However, the symplectic area and energy of holomorphic curves with positive ends on Reeb orbits of period $T$ is bounded by $\sum T_{\gamma^+} - \sum T_{\gamma^-}$. When attempting to capture the infinite spectrum of primes, the action periods $T_p = \log p \to \infty$ grow unbounded. By the SFT compactness theorem (Bourgeois-Eliashberg-Hofer-Wysocki-Zehnder), sequences of holomorphic curves of diverging energy do not converge to smooth curves, but instead degenerate into infinite holomorphic buildings with breaking of cylinders and continuous bubbling of planes and spheres. This infinite bubbling divergence destroys Fredholm regularity, causing the linearized contact homology complex to lose discrete grading and index control. Regularizing this divergence requires introducing ad-hoc energy cutoffs that truncate the prime sequence, instantly re-encountering the exact Heisenberg time barrier and preventing SFT from isolating off-line zeros without circular zero-freeness assumptions.
