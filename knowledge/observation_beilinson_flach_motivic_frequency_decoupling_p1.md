## Summary
In motivic cohomology and Euler systems (Beilinson, Flach, Kings, Loeffler, Zerbes), Beilinson–Flach elements $\mathcal{BF} \in H^3_{\mathrm{mot}}(Y_1(N) \times Y_1(N), \mathbb{Q}(2))$ are constructed from Eisenstein symbols on modular curves. Under regulator maps to Deligne cohomology, their values evaluate Rankin–Selberg $L$-values $L(f \otimes g, 1)$ of modular forms $f$ and $g$ at central integer points, yielding Euler systems for Galois representations.

However, Beilinson–Flach elements evaluate algebraic cycle classes in motivic cohomology specializing to discrete algebraic periods and integer values of $L$-functions. They operate in the algebraic motivic category, which decouples completely from the continuous transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of arithmetic Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, Beilinson–Flach motivic classes provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
