## Summary
In toric and birational geometry (Altmann, Hausen, Ilten, Süss, Fujita, Blum), $T$-varieties of complexity one admit an effective action of an algebraic torus $T$ of dimension $\dim(X) - 1$, described combinatorially by proper polyhedral divisors $\mathcal{D} = \sum \Delta_P \otimes P$ on a curve $Y$. K-stability and Fujita's valuative criterion $\beta(E) = A_X(E) - S(E)$ reduce to piecewise linear convex optimization problems on the divisorial fan, where stability thresholds $\delta(X, \mathcal{S})$ are computed via slice volumes of polyhedral complexes.

However, polyhedral divisorial fans and slice volume minimizers evaluate convex polyhedral cone volumes, Newton–Okounkov bodies, and combinatorial piecewise linear data of algebraic varieties over $\mathbb{C}$. They decouple completely from the discrete arithmetic prime distribution over $\mathbb{Z}$ and the infinite Euler product $\prod_p (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$. Consequently, complexity-one $T$-variety polyhedral fan invariants provide zero transverse analytical mechanism to detect or eliminate off-line complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$.
