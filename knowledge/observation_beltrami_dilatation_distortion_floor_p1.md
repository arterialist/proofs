## Summary
In geometric function theory and Teichmüller theory, conformal slit domains $\Omega = \mathbb{C} \setminus \bigcup_k S_k$ and extremal length methods attempt to detect off-line zeros $\lambda_0 = 1/4 - \gamma_0^2 + i\gamma_0(2\beta_0-1)$ by measuring geometric distortion under quasiconformal Beltrami deformations $\mu = \bar{\partial} f / \partial f$. If an off-line zero exists, it creates an isolated branch point or puncture in the slit plane. However, isolated points have logarithmic conformal capacity zero ($\operatorname{cap}(\{\lambda_0\}) = 0$).

When attempting to extract a macroscopic geometric obstruction, a localized Beltrami deformation concentrated on a disk $B(\lambda_0, \delta_0)$ produces a maximal dilatation $K = \frac{1+\|\mu\|_\infty}{1-\|\mu\|_\infty} = 1 + O(\delta_0)$. By the Grötzsch and Teichmüller extremal length inequalities, this $O(\delta_0)$ dilatation perturbation is completely absorbed by the continuous background slit capacity $\operatorname{cap}(\partial \Omega) = \infty$, yielding an analytic perturbation of magnitude at most $O(\delta_0^2 / \gamma_0^3)$. Furthermore, the geometric clearance between the off-line puncture and the infinite family of critical zeros collapses as $O(1/\gamma_0) \to 0$ as $\gamma_0 \to \infty$. As a result, the moduli of boundary curve families are asymptotically blind to off-line zeros, precluding any contradiction with conformal invariants.
