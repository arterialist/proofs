## Summary
In absolute algebraic geometry (Soulé, Deitmar, Connes–Consani), geometry over the "field with one element" $\mathbb{F}_1$ models schemes without additive structures, replacing commutative rings with commutative multiplicative monoids $(A, \cdot)$. Absolute zeta functions $\zeta_X(s)$ over $\mathbb{F}_1$ evaluate Euler products $\prod_{i} (1 - s)^{-a_i}$, having poles at discrete integers $s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\le \dim X}$ and zero complex oscillations.

However, removing the additive group structure strips away the destructive interference mechanism responsible for complex zero cancellation. The Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s}$ owes its non-trivial zeros $\rho = 1/2 + it$ to oscillatory cancellations among terms with imaginary exponents $n^{-it} = \cos(t \log n) - i \sin(t \log n)$. Absolute monoid schemes and $\mathbb{F}_1$-motives possess only multiplicative scaling symmetries with zero addition, yielding purely combinatorial polynomial invariants that are structurally blind to transcendental complex roots in the critical strip.
