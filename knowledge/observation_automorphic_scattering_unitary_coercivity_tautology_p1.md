## Summary
For the modular surface $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}$, the Eisenstein scattering matrix $\Phi(s) = \sqrt{\pi} \frac{\Gamma(s-1/2)\zeta(2s-1)}{\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)}$ satisfies the functional reflection equation $\Phi(s)\Phi(1-s) = I$. Along the critical line $s = 1/2 + it$, $\Phi(1/2+it)$ is strictly unitary: $|\Phi(1/2+it)| = 1$. Attempting to establish zero-freeness in $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1/2$ by deducing a coercive resolvent lower bound on the Laplacian $(s(1-s) - \Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}$ fails because the unitary scattering property on the line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$ is an exact functional symmetry ($U^* U = I$) holding independently of zero locations off the line. Off-line poles of $\Phi(s)$ correspond to zeros of $\zeta(2s)$; the resolvent identity $(s(1-s) - \Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1} = R_0(s) + \text{scattering terms}$ exhibits resonance poles without providing an internal coercive restoring force to exclude resonances off $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$. Thus, boundary unitarity provides zero forward coercivity off the critical line without externally assuming zero-freeness.
