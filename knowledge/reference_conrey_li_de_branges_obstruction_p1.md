## Key Result
For natural families of weight functions associated with the completed Riemann zeta function $\xi(s)$ (including gamma-factor quotients $\rho_\nu(t) = |\Gamma(1/4 + it/2)|^2$ and truncated Euler products), the positivity condition required for de Branges Hilbert spaces of entire functions $\mathcal{H}(E)$ fails. Specifically, the reproducing kernel matrix develops negative eigenvalues, and the associated Hamiltonian matrix $H(x)$ fails to remain positive semi-definite (e.g., $\operatorname{Re}[\xi(1 + 282i)/\xi(2 + 282i)] \approx -1.32 \times 10^{-4} < 0$).

## Hypotheses / Conditions
- The function $E(z) = A(z) - i B(z)$ must satisfy the Hermite-Biehler inequality $|E(z)| > |E(\bar{z})|$ for all $\operatorname{Im}(z) > 0$.
- The associated 2x2 Hamiltonian matrix $H(x)$ in the canonical system $J Y' = z H(x) Y$ must satisfy $H(x) \ge 0$ (positive semi-definiteness) almost everywhere on $[0, L)$.

## How It Applies
Proves that Louis de Branges's proposed program (1986–1994) to establish the Riemann Hypothesis via axiomatic Hilbert spaces of entire functions cannot succeed with Euler-product-based approximations or naive Gamma-weight regularizations. Any candidate claiming that $\xi(s)$ generates a de Branges space must prove that its specific regularized Hamiltonian bypasses these negative eigenvalues.

## Caveats
The result specifically refutes de Branges's original proposed choices of weight functions and natural approximations. It does not preclude the abstract existence of an unknown, non-perturbative canonical Hamiltonian, but proving that any candidate Hamiltonian is positive semi-definite is logically equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis itself.
