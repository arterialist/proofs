## Summary
In Donaldson–Thomas theory and supersymmetric moduli (Kontsevich–Soibelman, Joyce–Song), stability structures on triangulated categories define numerical or motivic BPS invariants $\Omega(\gamma; z) \in \mathbb{Q}$ parameterized by central charges $Z: K_0(\mathcal{D}) \to \mathbb{C}$. When crossing a wall of marginal stability $\mathcal{W}$ in stability space, the invariants jump according to the universal Lie-algebraic wall-crossing formula:
$$\prod_{\gamma \in \Gamma, \arg Z(\gamma) \searrow} S_\gamma = \prod_{\gamma \in \Gamma, \arg Z(\gamma) \nearrow} S_\gamma$$
where $S_\gamma = \exp\left( \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Omega(n\gamma)}{n^2} e_{n\gamma} \right)$ are formal Poisson automorphisms.

However, the Kontsevich–Soibelman wall-crossing identity is a purely combinatorial and algebraic factorisation identity in a formal Lie algebra. It expresses the conservation of BPS states under continuous deformation of categorical stability conditions, but carries zero analytic connection to univariate Dirichlet $L$-functions $\zeta(s)$ or their root distributions. A hypothetical off-line zero $\zeta(\sigma_0 + it_0) = 0$ with $\sigma_0 > 1/2$ exerts zero influence on categorical stability conditions or Stokes automorphisms $S_\gamma$. Treating wall-crossing factorizations as an analytic coercivity mechanism for horizontal zero-free regions conflates combinatorial state counting with transcendental complex function theory.
