## Summary
Griffin, Ono, Rolen, & Zagier (GORZ 2019) proved that for each fixed degree $d \ge 1$, the shifted Jensen polynomial $J_d^{X, n}(t)$ associated with the Taylor coefficients of $\Xi(\sqrt{x})$ has all real roots for all sufficiently large shifts $n \ge N(d)$, asymptotically converging to the Hermite polynomial $H_d(t)$. However, establishing the Riemann Hypothesis requires that for the unshifted origin $n = 0$, the Jensen polynomial $J_d^{X, 0}(t)$ has all real roots for *all* degrees $d \ge 1$ simultaneously. The GORZ theorem is strictly a horizontal limit ($n \to \infty$ along a fixed degree $d$); the required threshold satisfies $N(d) \to \infty$ as $d \to \infty$, providing zero effective uniform bound on the all-degree property at $n=0$. Furthermore, checking low degrees $d \le 4$ is subject to Haselgrove scale blindness, completely incapable of detecting off-line zeros at heights $\gamma > 3 \times 10^{12}$. Consequently, descending GORZ asymptotic hyperbolicity to the unshifted global setting carries 100% of Millennium RH analytical depth.
