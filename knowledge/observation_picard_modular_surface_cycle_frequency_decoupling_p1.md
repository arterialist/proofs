## Summary
In arithmetic algebraic geometry and unitary Shimura varieties (Picard, Kudla, Rapoport, Rogawski, Finis), Picard modular surfaces $X = \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{B}^2$ are Shimura surfaces associated to quasi-split or non-quasi-split unitary groups $U(2, 1)$ over imaginary quadratic fields $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$. Special algebraic cycles $C_m \subset X$ are totally geodesic complex curves attached to positive-definite hermitian vectors of norm $m$. By the Kudla–Rapoport program, generating series of these special cycles define modular forms of weight 3 for congruence subgroups of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, and their arithmetic intersection pairings evaluate central derivatives of Rankin–Selberg $L$-functions.

However, Picard modular special cycles and their arithmetic degrees evaluate algebraic cycle classes and intersection pairings attached to discrete hermitian norms $m \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$. They operate within the algebraic geometry of unitary Shimura varieties and automorphic representations on $U(2, 1)$, which decouples completely from the continuous vertical transcendental frequencies $t = \operatorname{Im}(s)$ of Dirichlet series $\zeta(s) = \sum n^{-s}$. Consequently, Picard modular cycle pairings provide zero transverse analytical coercivity to constrain the complex zeros of $\zeta(s)$ off $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.
