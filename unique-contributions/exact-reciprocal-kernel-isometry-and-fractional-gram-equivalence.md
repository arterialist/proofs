## 1. Executive Summary and Mathematical Statement

In the Nyman–Beurling approach to the Riemann hypothesis, one approximates the constant function $1$ on $(0, 1)$ by linear combinations of fractional parts $\rho(\theta / x) = \{\theta / x\}$. An alternative representation uses the reciprocal scaling $\frac{\theta}{x} \rho(x / \theta)$. While these two representations appear structurally different, their underlying inner-product geometries are completely identical.

This contribution proves:

1. **Exact $L^2(0, \infty)$ Reciprocal Distance Isometry:**  
   For any two finite linear combinations of fractional part kernels:
   \[
   F(x) = \sum_{i \in S} c_i \rho(\theta_i / x), \quad G(x) = \sum_{j \in T} d_j \rho(\eta_j / x),
   \]
   and their reciprocal conjugates:
   \[
   \widetilde{F}(x) = \sum_{i \in S} c_i \frac{\theta_i}{x} \rho(x / \theta_i), \quad \widetilde{G}(x) = \sum_{j \in T} d_j \frac{\eta_j}{x} \rho(x / \eta_j),
   \]
   the $L^2(0, \infty)$ distance is rigorously preserved:
   \[
   \int_0^\infty (F(x) - G(x))^2 dx = \int_0^\infty (\widetilde{F}(x) - \widetilde{G}(x))^2 dx.
   \]
2. **Pointwise Gram Kernel Reciprocal Invariance:**  
   For any parameters $\theta, \eta > 0$:
   \[
   \int_0^\infty \rho(\theta / x) \rho(\eta / x) dx = \int_0^\infty \left( \frac{\theta}{x} \rho(x / \theta) \right) \left( \frac{\eta}{x} \rho(x / \eta) \right) dx.
   \]
3. **Hyperbolic Involutive Change of Variables:**  
   The equality of Gram matrices is generated by the nonlinear involution:
   \[
   y = \frac{\theta \eta}{x}, \quad dx = -\frac{\theta \eta}{y^2} dy,
   \]
   under which $\theta / x = y / \eta$, $\eta / x = y / \theta$, and the Jacobian $\frac{\theta \eta}{y^2} = \left(\frac{\theta}{y}\right) \left(\frac{\eta}{y}\right)$ factors into the product of reciprocal weights.
4. **Finite Gram Matrix Isometry:**  
   For any index sets $S, T$ and coefficient vectors $c, d$:
   \[
   \langle F, G \rangle_{L^2(0, \infty)} = \langle \widetilde{F}, \widetilde{G} \rangle_{L^2(0, \infty)}.
   \]
5. **Universal $L^2$ Integrability of Kernel Products:**  
   For any $\theta, \eta > 0$, the kernel products:
   \[
   x \mapsto \rho(\theta / x) \rho(\eta / x) \quad \text{and} \quad x \mapsto \left( \frac{\theta}{x} \rho(x / \theta) \right) \left( \frac{\eta}{x} \rho(x / \eta) \right)
   \]
   are unconditionally integrable on $(0, \infty)$ without regularizing mollifiers, satisfying the uniform $O(x^{-2})$ decay bound as $x \to \infty$.

## 2. Mathematical Proof

### 2.1. Change of Variables
Let $f(y) = \frac{\theta \eta}{y}$ on $(0, \infty)$.
Then $f$ is a decreasing diffeomorphism of $(0, \infty)$ onto $(0, \infty)$ with derivative:
\[
f'(y) = -\frac{\theta \eta}{y^2}.
\]
Under $x = f(y)$:
\[
\frac{\theta}{x} = \frac{\theta}{\theta \eta / y} = \frac{y}{\eta}, \quad \frac{\eta}{x} = \frac{\eta}{\theta \eta / y} = \frac{y}{\theta}.
\]
Applying the measure-theoretic change of variables to $\int_0^\infty \rho(\theta / x) \rho(\eta / x) dx$:
\[
\int_0^\infty \rho(\theta / x) \rho(\eta / x) dx = \int_0^\infty \rho(y / \eta) \rho(y / \theta) \cdot \frac{\theta \eta}{y^2} dy.
\]
Splitting $\frac{\theta \eta}{y^2} = \left(\frac{\theta}{y}\right) \left(\frac{\eta}{y}\right)$:
\[
= \int_0^\infty \left( \frac{\eta}{y} \rho(y / \eta) \right) \left( \frac{\theta}{y} \rho(y / \theta) \right) dy = \int_0^\infty K_{\text{rec}}(\theta, y) K_{\text{rec}}(\eta, y) dy.
\]

### 2.2. Expansion of Squared Differences
Let $F = \sum_i c_i K_{\text{frac}}(\theta_i, \cdot)$ and $G = \sum_j d_j K_{\text{frac}}(\eta_j, \cdot)$.
Then:
\[
\|F - G\|^2_{L^2} = \langle F, F \rangle - 2 \langle F, G \rangle + \langle G, G \rangle.
\]
By linearity and Fubini's theorem for finite sums:
\[
\langle F, G \rangle = \sum_{i, j} c_i d_j \int_0^\infty K_{\text{frac}}(\theta_i, x) K_{\text{frac}}(\eta_j, x) dx = \sum_{i, j} c_i d_j \int_0^\infty K_{\text{rec}}(\theta_i, x) K_{\text{rec}}(\eta_j, x) dx = \langle \widetilde{F}, \widetilde{G} \rangle.
\]
Applying this identity to all three terms in the expansion gives $\|\widetilde{F} - \widetilde{G}\|^2_{L^2} = \|F - G\|^2_{L^2}$.
