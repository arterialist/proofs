# Actual cross-profile domains and weak score tests

Fix a finite core radius R>0. All objects below are the actual killed-theta objects in [complete ground covariance clock](theta-complete-ground-covariance-clock.md), [full cross arrival covariance repair](theta-full-cross-arrival-covariance-repair.md), and [prime component cross profile bound](theta-prime-component-cross-profile-bound.md). In particular D=L_O, Dψ=αψ, ψ is even, positive almost everywhere, normalized in L²(ν), and bounded. Write
$$
 \rho(u)=(e^{|u|}+1)^{-1},\quad d\gamma=\rho\psi\,d\nu,
 \quad a=\gamma(O),\quad \varepsilon=\operatorname{sgn}u.
$$
We use the real Hilbert-space notation; polarization gives the complex identities. On centered potentials the full ground form is generated by K=a+A_rem. Its form domain is exactly the original ground potential domain, with equivalent form norms. On all of L²(γ), the positive extension K acts as a on constants. No identification of its operator domain with the original-clock operator domain is assumed.

The complete component profiles satisfy
$$
 K\varepsilon=a\varepsilon+w_c+w_p,\qquad
 w_c=\varepsilon(T_c-a),\quad w_p=\varepsilon T_p,
$$
$$
 \varepsilon\in D(K),\quad w_c,w_p\in L²(\gamma),\quad
 \langle w_c,\varepsilon\rangle=d_c,
 \quad\langle w_p,\varepsilon\rangle=p.
 \tag{1}
$$
Here p=4P_R, c=2G(-1/2), and Δ=p-c² is the actual prime quadrature error. Every proper prime power remains in T_p and p.

## 1. The continuous profile is in the full operator domain

Put g(u)=Φ(u)ψ(u)1_(u>R) and G(s)=∫_R^∞e^(−su)g(u)du. The exact continuous cross kernel gives, for u>R,
$$
 T_c(u)=2\sum_{j\ge0}G(2j+1/2)e^{-2ju},\qquad a=2G(1/2).
 \tag{2}
$$
All terms are positive. Since G(2j+1/2)≤e^(−2jR)G(1/2), this series and each differentiated series converge uniformly on [R,∞). Let
$$
 b_R=(e^{4R}-1)^{-1},\quad B_R=ab_R,
 \quad L_R=\max\left\{
 \frac{2ae^{-4R}}{(1-e^{-4R})²},\frac{ab_R}{R}\right\}.
$$
Then the odd extension w_c on O satisfies
$$
 |w_c|\le B_R,\qquad
 |w_c(u)-w_c(v)|\le L_R|u-v|\quad(u,v\in O).
 \tag{3}
$$
For opposite halves, the latter bound follows from |u-v|≥2R. No extension through the deleted core is required.

Here is a domain argument retaining the continuous diagonal singularity. Let q_O(u,dv) be the original full internal theta jump rate. Its continuous part is Φ(v)r(|u-v|)dv/[2cosh(u/2)], and its entire prime departure is bounded by B_p. Consequently
$$
 C_1:=\sup_{u\in O}\int_O\min(|u-v|,1)q_O(u,dv)
 \le \Phi_*\int_0^\infty r(t)\min(t,1)dt+B_p<\infty.
 \tag{4}
$$
The kernel r has its actual 1/t singularity at zero and exponential tail. Set L_*=max(L_R,2B_R). The full ground conductance is Jψ(du,dv)=ψ(u)ψ(v)ν(du)q_O(u,dv). Equations (3)–(4), bounded ψ, and finite ν(O) imply finite full edge energy of w_c. Thus w_c belongs to the common complete form domain.

The absolutely convergent expression
$$
 V_c(u)=\frac1{\rho(u)}\int_O
       \psi(v)[w_c(u)-w_c(v)]q_O(u,dv)
 \tag{5}
$$
satisfies
$$
 |V_c(u)|\le \|\psi\|_\infty L_*C_1/\rho(u),\qquad
 \|V_c\|_\gamma²
 \le (\|\psi\|_\infty L_*C_1)²
                  \int_O\frac{\psi}{\rho}\,d\nu<\infty.
 \tag{6}
$$
The last integral is finite by bounded ψ and theta tails. Symmetry and absolute integrability identify the complete weak form with ⟨V_c,F⟩γ for every form test F. Oddness gives zero γ mean. Therefore
$$
 \boxed{\quad w_c\in D(K),\qquad Kw_c=V_c.\quad}
 \tag{7}
$$
This proves an actual full-operator domain statement; it does not replace the prime jumps by a continuous rate or require regularity of ψ beyond its proved bound.

The continuous profile also supplies the explicit positive mixed Gram estimate
$$
 0<\langle w_p,w_c\rangle_\gamma
 =\int T_p(T_c-a)d\gamma
 \le ab_R p=ab_R(c²+\Delta).
 \tag{8}
$$
Strict positivity uses actual positive prime crossing mass and T_c-a>0. In particular Δ cannot be used alone for this mixed term: the full positive mass p occurs. Moreover
$$
 d_c=4\sum_{j\ge1}G(2j+1/2)²,\qquad
 \langle w_p,w_c\rangle
 =\langle\varepsilon,Kw_c\rangle-ad_c-\|w_c\|².
 \tag{9}
$$
The second identity follows from (1) and self-adjointness using (7). It is an exact legitimate weak test of the full sign equation.

## 2. The actual score quotient is an operator test

Let λ=1/2, δ=λ−α, σ=Φ′/Φ, y=σ_O, q=τ_O, and f=Bσ_C. The actual score and hard-projection domain identities in [true affine odd score calibration](theta-true-affine-odd-score-calibration.md) give
$$
 y\in D(D),\qquad
 (D-\alpha)y=f+\delta y-\frac\lambda2q.
 \tag{10}
$$
All these functions are odd. Put S_z=z/ρ for each weighted source z and
$$
 \varphi_y=y/\psi.
$$
Since y∈D(D), the original ground transform places φ_y in its form domain: its L²(ψ²ν) norm is ||y||ν and its edge energy is ⟨(D−α)y,y⟩ν. The proved common-domain theorem then puts φ_y in the complete γ form domain. In particular φ_y∈L²(γ), even though no lower bound on ψ has been imposed. Its γ mean is zero by parity.

The proved weighted crossing bound and theta score bounds give S_f,S_y,S_q∈L²(γ). For every common-domain test F, the original operator equation (10) yields
$$
 \mathsf E(\varphi_y,F)
 =\langle(D-\alpha)y,\psi F\rangle_\nu
 =\left\langle S_f+\delta S_y-\frac\lambda2S_q,F\right\rangle_\gamma.
$$
The representation theorem therefore proves
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \varphi_y\in D(K),\qquad
 K\varphi_y=S_f+\delta S_y-\frac\lambda2S_q.
 \quad}
 \tag{11}
$$
In particular the continuous-profile test is entirely unregularized:
$$
 \langle S_f,w_c\rangle+\delta\langle S_y,w_c\rangle
 -\frac\lambda2\langle S_q,w_c\rangle
 =\langle\varphi_y,Kw_c\rangle.
 \tag{12}
$$
Every term is finite. Formula (11), rather than a pointwise differentiated ground state, also supplies the domain input for jointly retaining ε and φ_y in the positive component-rank construction.

## 3. Prime-profile tests without an extra domain hypothesis

The following weak-pairing argument uses only $w_p\in L^2(\gamma)$. The [full-form proof](theta-prime-profile-full-form-domain.md) now proves the stronger membership for the actual profile. The separate weak argument remains valid without that input. By (1), (7), and ε∈D(K), that assertion is exactly equivalent to
$$
 K\varepsilon\in D(K^{1/2}),\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad
 \varepsilon\in D(K^{3/2}).
 \tag{13}
$$
The separated cross-rate L² estimate proves only ε∈D(K). It cannot supply (13) by itself.

For η>0 set z_η=(I+ηK)^(−1)w_p. Then z_η∈D(K), is odd, and converges to w_p in L²(γ). For each F∈D(K),
$$
 \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\mathsf E(F,z_\eta)
 =\langle KF,w_p\rangle.
 \tag{14}
$$
This supplies, in particular, the exact score-source identity
$$
 \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}
 \left\langle\varphi_y,
       K(I+\eta K)^{-1}w_p\right\rangle
 =\langle S_f,w_p\rangle+\delta\langle S_y,w_p\rangle
      -\frac\lambda2\langle S_q,w_p\rangle,
 \tag{15}
$$
and the full sign/profile Gram limit
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\mathsf E(\varepsilon,z_\eta)
 =a(c²+\Delta)+\langle w_c,w_p\rangle+\|w_p\|².
 \quad}
 \tag{16}
$$
It retains the arithmetic mass error, the actual mixed Gram, and the entire prime-profile variance. The source-side limits in (15) need only the established L² facts.

If μ_p is the spectral measure of w_p for K, then
$$
 \mathsf E(z_\eta)
 =\int\frac{t}{(1+\eta t)²}\,d\mu_p(t)
 \uparrow\int t\,d\mu_p(t).
 \tag{17}
$$
For a general $L^2$ profile the limit can be infinite unless (13) is proved. The linked actual-profile theorem proves (13), so its limit is finite. Thus (14)–(16) are convergent weak pairings with specified operator-domain vectors, and their proof alone does not assert convergence in form norm. The stronger linked theorem supplies finite energy for the actual prime profile. They impose actual source compatibility but do not establish a sign for Δ or the unresolved full affine inequality.

## 4. The actual pole quotient is a second operator-domain test

The original full jump measure is
$$
 \eta(dt)=r(|t|)dt+\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
       (\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n})(dt),\qquad
 r(t)=\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}.
$$
Write b(u)=2cosh(u/2) and w(u)=Φ(u)/b(u). For the actual pole function τ(u)=tanh(u/2), the full action is the absolutely convergent function
$$
 \ell_\tau(u):=(L\tau)(u)
 =\frac1{b(u)}\int\Phi(u+t)[\tau(u)-\tau(u+t)]\eta(dt).
 \tag{18}
$$
Indeed |τ(u)−τ(u+t)|≤min(|t|/2,2), so the continuous diagonal is integrable. If B_p bounds the full original prime departure rate, then
$$
 \|\ell_\tau\|_\infty\le C_\tau
 :=\Phi_*\int_0^\infty r(t)\min(t/2,2)dt+2B_p
 \le\tfrac52\Phi_*+2B_p<\infty.
 \tag{19}
$$
For the final inequality, expand r(t)=Σ_(j≥0)e^(−(2j+1/2)t). Positivity gives ∫tr(t)dt=Σ_(j≥0)(2j+1/2)^(−2)≤4+∫_0^∞(2x+1/2)^(−2)dx=5. This uses the full prime departure bound, with no cutoff of prime powers. Boundedness and the weak jump identity also directly verify the established τ∈D(L).

Pairing positive and negative displacements gives a useful explicit version of the full action:
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \ell_\tau(u)=\frac2{b(u)²}\left\{
  \int_0^\infty\frac{w(u-t)-w(u+t)}{1+e^{-t}}dt
  +\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)(1-1/n)
          [w(u-\log n)-w(u+\log n)]\right\}.
 \quad}
 \tag{20}
$$
Here τ(u)−τ(u+t)=−4sinh(t/2)/[b(u)b(u+t)] and sinh(t/2)r(t)=1/[2(1+e^(−t))]. Absolute convergence was established before this regrouping. In particular (20) is a full arithmetic action, not a scalar eigenvalue assertion for τ.

Let q=τ_O and q_C=τ_C. The hard split gives
$$
 s_q:=(D-\alpha)q=(\ell_\tau)_O+Bq_C-\alpha q.
 \tag{21}
$$
The sign of the crossing term is positive in this operator identity because the off-diagonal block of L is −B. The weighted source S_(s_q)=s_q/ρ belongs to L²(γ). For the bounded first and third terms this follows from
$$
 \|((\ell_\tau)_O-\alpha q)/\rho\|_\gamma²
 \le(C_\tau+\alpha)²\int_O\frac{\psi}{\rho}d\nu<\infty.
$$
The term Bq_C/ρ is admissible by the proved weighted crossing theorem for every core L² input.

Now put φ_q=q/ψ. The original ground transform and q∈D(D) give φ_q in the original potential form domain. The exact common-domain theorem places it in L²(γ) and the complete form domain. It is odd and hence centered. For every common-domain test F,
$$
 \mathsf E(\varphi_q,F)
 =\langle(D-\alpha)q,\psi F\rangle_\nu
 =\langle s_q/\rho,F\rangle_\gamma.
$$
Consequently
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \varphi_q=q/\psi\in D(K),\qquad
 K\varphi_q=s_q/\rho
 =S_{Bq_C}+S_{(\ell_\tau)_O}-\alpha S_q.
 \quad}
 \tag{22}
$$
No lower bound on ψ, differentiability of ψ, or unproved operator-domain equivalence is used.

Its original energy tends to zero on growing cores. If b_R bounds ||B_R|| and ν_R=ν(O_R), then
$$
 \begin{split}
 0\le E_q&:=\langle s_q,q\rangle_\nu\\
 &=\langle(\ell_\tau)_O,q\rangle
   +\langle Bq_C,q\rangle-\alpha\|q\|²\\
 &\le C_\tau\nu_R+b_R\sqrt{\nu(C_R)\nu_R}\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{23}
$$
The first inequality follows from the ground spectral bound. The upper estimate retains both mixed terms before applying Cauchy--Schwarz and discarding the nonpositive last term. It uses |τ|≤1, the fixed global constant Cτ, theta tail ν_R→0, and the proved b_R→0. At R=2 the accepted ν_R<10^(−47), b_R<10^(−20), and ν(C_R)≤1 give the explicit bound
$$
 E_q<C_\tau10^{-47}+10^{-43}.
 \tag{24}
$$
No numerical estimate of B_p beyond its proved finiteness is inserted in (24).

For completeness, the exact two-column energy pairing is finite and satisfies
$$
 \langle K\varphi_y,\varphi_q\rangle_\gamma
 =\langle(D-\alpha)y,q\rangle_\nu
 =\langle y,(D-\alpha)q\rangle_\nu,
\qquad
 |\langle(D-\alpha)y,q\rangle|²\le E_yE_q.
 \tag{25}
$$
Thus both actual quotient directions are legitimate simultaneously. Matching their K-actions in a positive covariance comparison would preserve their exact cross Gram as well as their separate energies. Small E_y and E_q alone do not bound inverse covariance on S_y,S_q: the matched source in (22) is s_q/ρ, not q/ρ.
