# Full cross-arrival profile and the true odd covariance

The full sign conductance gives a lower bound for the odd covariance. To obtain an upper bound, one must also measure how the actual cross-arrival profile differs from the score source. The resulting identity below retains every prime-power crossing and bounds its orthogonal residual by the same-half continuous budget. This residual bound requires no bridge-mass estimate.

## The sign potential belongs to the full clock operator domain

Fix $C=[-R,R]$, $O=O_+\cup O_-$, $R>0$, and the actual even normalized killed ground state $\psi$. Use
$$
 d\nu=A\Phi\,du,\quad A(u)=2\cosh(u/2),\quad
 \rho(u)=\frac1{e^{|u|}+1},\quad
 d\gamma=\rho\psi\,d\nu,\quad a=\gamma(O).
$$
The exact complete covariance clock is proved in [complete ground covariance clock](theta-complete-ground-covariance-clock.md). Its complete form on $\gamma$-centered potentials is generated by
$$
 K=a+A_{\rm rem}.
 \tag{1}
$$
The complete continuous-remainder estimate gives, on this centered space,
$$
 K\ge a+\eta>0
 \tag{2}
$$
for the fixed-core $\eta$ in [continuous remainder explicit gap](theta-continuous-remainder-explicit-gap.md), or its positive lower enclosure from [groundstate explicit mass and gap bounds](theta-groundstate-explicit-mass-and-gap-bounds.md).

There is a stronger inverse comparison for the residuals used below. Set
$$
 h=e^R-1,\qquad
 \kappa_{\rm half}=a+\frac{ha}{2}
                  =\frac{a(e^R+1)}2,
$$
and let $P_{1,\varepsilon}$ be the orthogonal projection in $L^2(\gamma)$ onto functions constant on each exterior half-line. The actual same-half continuous remainder minorant gives
$$
 E_{\rm rem}(F)\ge\frac{ha}{2}
             \|(I-P_{1,\varepsilon})F\|_\gamma^2.
 \tag{2a}
$$
It holds on the full closed form domain and retains every other edge as a nonnegative form. Thus the positive extension $K=a+A_{\rm rem}$ to all of $L^2(\gamma)$ satisfies the form-order bound
$$
 K\ge K_0:=aI+\frac{ha}{2}(I-P_{1,\varepsilon}).
$$
Here $K_0$ is bounded and strictly positive; its form domain is the whole Hilbert space, while the domain of $K$'s form is contained in it. Variational inversion therefore yields
$$
 K^{-1}\le K_0^{-1}
 =\frac1aP_{1,\varepsilon}
       +\frac1{\kappa_{\rm half}}(I-P_{1,\varepsilon}).
 \tag{2b}
$$
For example apply the inequality of energies to the supremum
$2\operatorname{Re}\langle e,F\rangle-\langle KF,F\rangle$,
then enlarge the test domain to that of $K_0$.
Consequently
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \langle K^{-1}e,e\rangle_\gamma
 \le\frac{\|e\|_\gamma^2}{\kappa_{\rm half}}
 \quad\text{if }e\perp1,\varepsilon.
 \quad}
 \tag{2c}
$$
No invariance of $\{1,\varepsilon\}^{\perp}$ under $K$ is asserted or needed. The inverse comparison retains any coupling of this space to the sign mode. The denominator improves $a+\eta$, since the two-cell gap used in (2) obeys $\eta\le ha/2$.

Let $\varepsilon(u)=\operatorname{sgn}u$ on $O$. It is centered in $\gamma$. Write the original internal cross-half arrival operator as
$$
 (\mathcal K_\times f)(u)=
 \int_{\substack{v\in O\\uv<0}}f(v)\,q_O(u,dv),
 \tag{3}
$$
where $q_O$ is the original full theta jump rate. Define the actual even positive profile
$$
 T(u)=\frac{2\mathcal K_\times\psi(u)}{\rho(u)}.
 \tag{4}
$$
Then
$$
 \boxed{\qquad
 \varepsilon\in D(K),\qquad K\varepsilon=\varepsilon T.
 \qquad}
 \tag{5}
$$

Here is the domain justification, including the continuous infinite activity. Let
$$
 P_\infty\ge\|\psi\|_\infty,\quad
 B_{\rm p}\ge\|b_{\rm prime}\|_\infty,\quad
 \Phi_*=\sup\Phi,\quad
 C_\times=B_{\rm p}+\frac{\Phi_*}{2}\int_{2R}^{\infty}r(t)\,dt.
 \tag{6}
$$
The integral is finite because $R>0$. Opposite-half continuous jumps have length at least $2R$, so their original departure rate is at most the second term of $C_\times$. The full prime cross rate is at most $B_{\rm p}$. Thus
$$
 0<\mathcal K_\times\psi\le P_\infty C_\times,\qquad
 \|T\|_\gamma^2
 \le4P_\infty^3 C_\times^2
       \int_O\rho^{-1}\,d\nu<\infty.
 \tag{7}
$$
The last integral is finite by theta decay. Strict positivity follows from the continuous cross kernel and $\psi>0$ almost everywhere.

Only opposite-half edges contribute to the energy of $\varepsilon$. Their mass is finite by the same estimates, so $\varepsilon$ belongs to the complete form domain. For any centered form test $F$, symmetry of the directed full ground-state conductance $J_\psi$ gives
$$
 \frac12\iint
 [\varepsilon(u)-\varepsilon(v)]\,
 \overline{[F(u)-F(v)]}\,J_\psi(du,dv)
 =\langle\varepsilon T,F\rangle_\gamma.
 \tag{8}
$$
The separated cross rate and (7) make the right side a bounded $L^2(\gamma)$ functional. They also justify the rearrangement in (8) by Cauchy--Schwarz after summing the two orientations. On same-half edges the increment of $\varepsilon$ is exactly zero, so no finite total rate is asserted for the full continuous process. The representation theorem now proves (5). The output $\varepsilon T$ is centered by parity.

## Exact all-prime formula and sign-energy factors

For $u>R$, formula (4) is
$$
 \begin{split}
 T(u)={}&
 2\int_{-\infty}^{-R}
       \frac{e^{v/2}\Phi(v)\psi(v)}
            {1-e^{-2(u-v)}}\,dv\\
 &+2\sum_{\substack{n\ge2\\ \log n>u+R}}
      \Lambda(n)e^{(u-\log n)/2}
      \Phi(u-\log n)\psi(u-\log n).
 \end{split}
 \tag{9}
$$
Its values on the negative half-line are obtained by even reflection. The first line is the entire continuous cross arrival, not its leading large-distance approximation. The second line includes every prime power satisfying the actual cross condition. One may instead extend $\psi$ by zero on $C$ and sum all negative-shift arrivals. Values at the countably many boundary coincidences do not affect any integral against $\gamma$.

For almost every $u$, the positive sum in (9) is finite as an absolute sum by the complete bounded original prime departure rate and bounded $\psi$. No estimate treats a general prime translation as an $L^2$-to-$L^\infty$ operator.

Let $J_{\rm cross}=J_\psi(O_+,O_-)$, using the directed conductance and just this one rectangle. The exact sign energy is
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 E_s=\langle K\varepsilon,\varepsilon\rangle_\gamma
      =\int_O T\,d\gamma
      =4J_{\rm cross}>0.
 \quad}
 \tag{10}
$$
Indeed the two opposite rectangles each have the same mass, their squared sign increment is four, and the form has prefactor $1/2$.

## An exact covariance identity and an explicit residual upper bound

Put $q=\tau_O$, and define
$$
 S_q=q/\rho,\qquad
 H=\langle S_q,\varepsilon\rangle_\gamma
  =\langle q,\psi\varepsilon\rangle_\nu>0,\qquad
 t=\frac H{E_s}.
 \tag{11}
$$
The source $S_q$ belongs to $L^2(\gamma)$ by boundedness of $\psi$ and theta decay. Define its actual residual
$$
 e=S_q-tK\varepsilon,\qquad
 \langle e,\varepsilon\rangle_\gamma=0.
 \tag{12}
$$
Both terms are odd, so $e$ is also centered and belongs to $L^2(\gamma)$.

The complete-clock covariance identity and $K^{-1}K\varepsilon=\varepsilon$ give
$$
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal C(q,q)
 &=\langle K^{-1}(tK\varepsilon+e),tK\varepsilon+e\rangle_\gamma\\
 &=t^2E_s+\langle K^{-1}e,e\rangle_\gamma.
 \end{split}
$$
The mixed term vanishes by (12). Hence
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal C(q,q)=\frac{H^2}{E_s}
                 +\langle K^{-1}e,e\rangle_\gamma,\qquad
 \frac{H^2}{E_s}\le\mathcal C(q,q)
 \le\frac{H^2}{E_s}+\frac{\|e\|_\gamma^2}{\kappa_{\rm half}}.
 \quad}
 \tag{13}
$$
There is no unresolved Green operator in the last upper bound. The residual is centered and orthogonal to $\varepsilon$, so (2c) applies. The earlier bound with denominator $a+\eta$ remains valid but is weaker.

Its residual is an explicit weighted profile difference. Put
$$
 Z(u)=e^{|u|}-1,\qquad
 S_q=\varepsilon Z,\qquad
 H=\int Z\,d\gamma,\quad E_s=\int T\,d\gamma.
$$
Then
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \|e\|_\gamma^2=\int_O
       \left(Z-\frac{\int Z\,d\gamma}{\int T\,d\gamma}T\right)^2d\gamma.
 \quad}
 \tag{14}
$$
The scalar difference inside this integral has zero $\gamma$-mean. Formula (14) measures the shape of the full cross-arrival profile, not only its total conductance. Sign conductance alone controls the first term in (13) from below; it cannot discard the nonnegative residual covariance.

## The two-source consequence for the true score calibration

For the actual exterior score $y=\sigma_O$, $S_y=y/\rho$ also belongs to $L^2(\gamma)$. Indeed $\psi$ is bounded, $\sigma$ has at most exponential growth, and $\nu$ has theta tails. Define
$$
 H_y=\langle S_y,\varepsilon\rangle_\gamma,\qquad
 e_y=S_y-\frac{H_y}{E_s}K\varepsilon.
$$
The same decomposition applies jointly to $y,q$. With
$H_q=H$, $e_q=e$, their covariance matrix has the exact form
$$
 \mathcal C(f_i,f_j)=
 \frac{H_i\overline{H_j}}{E_s}
       +\langle K^{-1}e_i,e_j\rangle_\gamma,
 \qquad f_i,f_j\in\{y,q\}.
 \tag{15}
$$
Thus it is bounded above, as a positive Hermitian form on the two-source span, by the explicit matrix
$$
 \overline C_{ij}=
 \frac{H_i\overline{H_j}}{E_s}
       +\frac{\langle e_i,e_j\rangle_\gamma}{\kappa_{\rm half}}.
 \tag{16}
$$
This is a joint covariance bound, not an entrywise bound on a signed mixed covariance. Every linear combination of $e_y,e_q$ is orthogonal to both $1$ and $\varepsilon$, so (2c) proves the matrix inequality on their entire span.

It has a direct consumer in [true affine odd score calibration](theta-true-affine-odd-score-calibration.md). That exact identity writes, for $\delta=\lambda-\alpha$,
$$
 F_\alpha[\sigma_C]
 =\delta\|\sigma\|^2-\frac{\lambda}{4}
 -\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathcal C}(\delta y,1/2).
$$
Apply the positive-form upper bound (16) before affine minimization. Since $\overline C_{qq}>0$, this gives
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 F_\alpha[\sigma_C]\ge
 \delta\|\sigma\|^2-\frac{\lambda}{4}
 -\delta^2\overline C_{yy}
 +\frac{|1/2+\delta\overline C_{yq}|^2}{\overline C_{qq}}.
 \quad}
 \tag{17}
$$
All quantities on the right are full cross-arrival integrals, source norms, and the explicit denominator $a(e^R+1)/2$. The exact vector $K\varepsilon$, including every prime cross arrival, remains in each residual before the same-half comparison is applied. Thus this is not a return to a continuous-only covariance majorant for the original sources. Neither the mixed $\delta$ term nor the $\delta^2$ residual is assumed small. The actual residuals (14)--(16) have not been estimated sharply enough here to make (17) nonnegative at $R=2$ or at another specified core.

## Attribution and formal scope

The closed-form variational inverse, nonnegative-form Cauchy–Schwarz, finite-rank inverse identities and affine completion of squares used here are classical. The actual theta ground state, full continuous kernel, every prime power and stated fixed-core domains remain part of the theorem. These are written proofs; no Lean formalization of this profile comparison or evaluation of the required arithmetic smallness is asserted.
