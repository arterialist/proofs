# Higher-dimensional unitary prime phases and the exact defect domain

Let $U:\mathbb N\to\mathcal U(\mathcal H)$ be a completely multiplicative unitary representation:
$$
U(1)=I,\qquad U(mn)=U(m)U(n).
\tag{1}
$$
The operators commute. For $v\in\mathcal H$, define the actual half-neighbor defect as a nonnegative extended sum
$$
\mathfrak E_2(v)=\sum_{n\ge1}
\|[U(2n+1)-U(2)U(n)]v\|^2
=\sum_{n\ge1}\|[U(2n+1)-U(2n)]v\|^2.
\tag{2}
$$
No continuous scale parameter or self-adjoint generator is assumed in (1). This note gives a written proof by scalar rigidity and the joint spectral theorem. It is not a Lean formalization, and no priority claim is made for the representation argument.

**Theorem.** If $v\ne0$ and $\mathfrak E_2(v)<\infty$, then on the reducing cyclic subspace
$$
\mathcal H_v=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{U(a)U(b)^*v:a,b\in\mathbb N\}},
$$
there is a self-adjoint operator $T$ such that
$$
\boxed{U(n)|_{\mathcal H_v}=e^{i(\log n)T}\quad(n\ge1).}
\tag{3}
$$
On this subspace the exact energy is
$$
\mathfrak E_2(w)=\|\psi(T)^{1/2}w\|^2,
\qquad
\psi(t)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\left[1-\cos\left(t\log\left(1+\frac1{2n}\right)\right)\right],
\tag{4}
$$
with both sides interpreted as infinity outside the form domain. Moreover
$$
\boxed{D(\mathfrak E_2|_{\mathcal H_v})=\operatorname{Dom}|T|^{1/2},}
\tag{5}
$$
and the corresponding form norms are equivalent. If finite-energy vectors are dense in the whole space, then (3)-(5) hold on the whole space. A hypothesis on one vector only controls its reducing cyclic subspace.

## 1. The scalar input and its precise source

The previously proved scalar statement is
$$
\mathcal E_2(\chi)<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi(n)=n^{it}\text{ for some }t\in\mathbb R,
\tag{6}
$$
for completely multiplicative $\chi:\mathbb N\to\mathbb T$. This uses the half-neighbor bridge from [half-neighbor phase rigidity](../geometry/half-neighbor-phase-rigidity.md), followed by [Wirsing-Zagier, *Multiplicative functions with difference tending to zero*, Theorem 1 and its multiplicative formulation, printed pp.75-77](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.4064/aa100-1-6/fulltext.pdf). Its theorem identifies unimodular multiplicative functions whose consecutive ratios tend to one.

For completeness, the arithmetic bridge is short. If $a_n=\chi(2n+1)-\chi(2n)$, complete multiplicativity and $(2n+1)^2-1=4n(n+1)$ give
$$
|\chi(2n+2)-\chi(2n+1)|
\le |a_n|+|a_{2n(n+1)}|.
$$
The index map on the right is injective. Thus finite $\sum|a_n|^2$ implies finite full adjacent energy, and in particular consecutive ratios tend to one. The cited scalar theorem applies. Conversely, for $\chi(n)=n^{it}$, each observed difference has modulus at most $|t|/(2n)$, proving finite energy. Unitarity is essential to this scalar step.

## 2. Joint spectral measure and the Borel character set

The countable commuting unitaries $U(p)$, indexed by primes, have their joint spectral representation on the compact prime torus
$$
X=\mathbb T^{\{p:\ p\text{ prime}\}}.
$$
For $x\in X$, write $\chi_x(n)=\prod_p x_p^{v_p(n)}$. In the cyclic representation generated by $v$, there is a finite positive measure $\mu_v$ on $X$ identifying $\mathcal H_v$ with $L^2(X,\mu_v)$, $v$ with the constant function one, and $U(n)$ with multiplication by $\chi_x(n)$. The total mass is $\|v\|^2$. This is the usual joint spectral theorem, applied to the abelian unitary representation of the positive rational group generated by the $U(p)$ and their inverses.

Positive Fubini applied directly to the finite partial sums in (2) gives
$$
\mathfrak E_2(v)=\int_X\mathcal E_2(\chi_x)\,d\mu_v(x).
\tag{7}
$$
If the left side is finite, the scalar energy is finite almost everywhere. By (6), the measure is concentrated on
$$
J=\{j(t):t\in\mathbb R\},\qquad j(t)=(p^{it})_p.
\tag{8}
$$

The required measurability can be checked explicitly. The map $j$ is continuous and injective. Indeed, if $j(t)=j(s)$ and $t\ne s$, the equations at primes two and three would make $\log2/\log3$ rational, contradicting unique factorization. The restriction of $j$ to every compact interval $[-N,N]$ is a homeomorphism onto its compact image. Hence $J$ is a countable union of compact sets and is Borel. Its inverse $t:J\to\mathbb R$ is Borel, as can be checked on those compact images.

Only measure concentration $\mu_v(X\setminus J)=0$ is asserted. It is not an assertion that the topological support is contained in $J$, or that this one-parameter subgroup is closed in the prime torus. Replacing concentration by either of those statements would be unjustified.

Transfer $\mu_v$ to a measure on $\mathbb R$ using this inverse. Multiplication by the real coordinate is a self-adjoint operator $T$, and the transformed operators are multiplication by $n^{it}$. This proves (3). It also supplies the continuous positive-scale extension $U(a)=e^{i(\log a)T}$ on the cyclic subspace.

For a global version, let $P$ denote the joint spectral projection measure. Every finite-energy vector has scalar spectral measure concentrated on $J$, so it lies in the closed range of $P(J)$. If these vectors are dense, then $P(J)=I$. The same Borel functional calculus defines $T$ on the whole Hilbert space, allowing arbitrary spectral multiplicities. No global conclusion follows for an orthogonal reducing subspace unobserved by the chosen vector.

## 3. The low-frequency energy

Set $a_n=\log(1+1/(2n))$. These numbers are positive and at most $1/(2n)$. Therefore (4) converges uniformly on compact real $t$-intervals, and defines an even continuous nonnegative function. Taylor's formula, with the summable fourth-order remainder, gives
$$
\psi(t)=c_2t^2+O(t^4),\qquad
c_2=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n^2\in(0,\pi^2/24],
\qquad t\to0.
\tag{9}
$$
Also $\psi(t)>0$ for every $t\ne0$. For sufficiently large $n$, the number $|t|a_n$ lies strictly between zero and $2\pi$, so its summand is positive.

## 4. The exact high-frequency constant

Let $\tau=|t|\to\infty$. On a fixed range $\epsilon\tau\le n\le R\tau$, with $0<\epsilon<R<\infty$,
$$
\tau a_n=\frac1{2(n/\tau)}+O\left(\frac1{\epsilon^2\tau}\right).
$$
The error is uniform, so the ordinary Riemann-sum limit on that range gives
$$
\frac2\tau\sum_{\epsilon\tau\le n\le R\tau}
 [1-\cos(\tau a_n)]
\longrightarrow 2\int_\epsilon^R
 \left[1-\cos\left(\frac1{2y}\right)\right]dy.
\tag{10}
$$
The omitted small-index block has normalized size at most $4\epsilon+O(\tau^{-1})$, since each full summand of $\psi$ is at most four. For the large-index block, $2(1-\cos x)\le x^2$ gives
$$
\frac2\tau\sum_{n>R\tau}[1-\cos(\tau a_n)]
\le \frac\tau4\sum_{n>R\tau}n^{-2}=O(R^{-1}).
\tag{11}
$$
The corresponding integral tails have the same bounds. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and $R\to\infty$ after taking the limit in (10). Substitution $u=1/(2y)$ yields
$$
\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{|t|}
=\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}\,du=\frac\pi2.
\tag{12}
$$
One entirely positive evaluation of the last integral uses
$u^{-2}=\int_0^\infty s e^{-su}ds$. Tonelli then turns it into
$\int_0^\infty(1+s^2)^{-1}ds=\pi/2$.

Combining (9), (12), and positivity and continuity on the intervening compact sets proves the two-sided comparison
$$
\boxed{\psi(t)\asymp\frac{t^2}{1+|t|},\qquad
1+\psi(t)\asymp1+|t|.}
\tag{13}
$$
The second comparison includes the ambient Hilbert norm. The first comparison cannot be replaced by $\psi(t)\asymp|t|$ near zero.

## 5. The exact vector form and its domain

For the self-adjoint generator obtained above, let $\mu_w$ be its scalar spectral measure at an arbitrary vector $w$. Positive Fubini again gives
$$
\mathfrak E_2(w)=\int_{\mathbb R}\psi(t)\,d\mu_w(t).
\tag{14}
$$
This proves (4), including infinite values, and makes the defect a closed nonnegative quadratic form. Equation (13) now gives
$$
\boxed{\|w\|^2+\mathfrak E_2(w)
\asymp \|w\|^2+\||T|^{1/2}w\|^2.}
\tag{15}
$$
Thus its form domain is exactly $\operatorname{Dom}|T|^{1/2}$. The weaker behavior at bounded $t$ causes no domain discrepancy because $\mu_w$ is a finite measure.

Conversely, every self-adjoint $T$ defines a unitary completely multiplicative model by (3), and (14)-(15) hold in it. Its finite-energy vectors are dense. In finite dimension this gives simultaneous diagonal scale phases with possibly several real parameters, or equivalently one self-adjoint matrix $T$. Higher dimension permits spectral multiplicity and distributions of scale parameters; under the stated energy hypothesis it does not permit unrelated unitary phases at different primes.

## Scope for the counting/division and theta problems

This is a theorem about the unitary multiplicative sector, coupled to the actual additive neighbor indices through (2). It does not construct a successor operator satisfying the full affine relation. The unilateral counting-space prime maps are isometries rather than unitaries, so the theorem cannot be applied to them without an additional representation argument that retains the boundary at one.

The theta zero expressions $n^{i\zeta}$ are nonunitary when $\operatorname{Im}\zeta\ne0$. Their finite-defect strip remains $|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<1/2$ for both conjugate factors. Imposing unitarity on those expressions would impose the desired midpoint in advance. No equivalence between this successor-division form and the actual Weil or theta jump form has been established. In particular its high-frequency multiplier $\psi(t)\sim(\pi/2)|t|$ differs from the logarithmic archimedean multiplier in the actual theta form. The dimensional lift supplies a representation and a precise regularity condition, with no claim of xi positivity or RH.

## The exact counting representation has no finite-defect vector

The preceding scope warning can be made quantitative. Let
$\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\ge1})$ with basis $(e_k)_{k\ge1}$.
For every $m\ge1$, allow any support-preserving isometry of the form

$$
V_m e_k=u_m(k)e_{mk},\qquad |u_m(k)|=1.
\tag{16}
$$

The raw exact-multiplication map has $u_m(k)=1$; compatible choices of
$u_m$ also include prime-phase gauges and all their proper powers.
No semigroup compatibility is needed for the following obstruction.
Write $T_N(v)=\sum_{k\ge N}|v_k|^2$. Then, for every $v\in\mathcal H$,

$$
\boxed{\left|\langle V_{2n+1}v,V_{2n}v\rangle\right|
\le T_{2n}(v)\longrightarrow0,}
\tag{17}
$$

and consequently

$$
\boxed{\left|\|[V_{2n+1}-V_{2n}]v\|^2-2\|v\|^2\right|
\le2T_{2n}(v)\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{18}
$$

In particular,
$\sum_{n\ge1}\|[V_{2n+1}-V_{2n}]v\|^2=\infty$
for every $v\ne0$. For a vector supported on $k\le K$, each defect
square is exactly $2\|v\|^2$ as soon as $2n>K$.

Here is the complete proof. An overlap of basis images requires
$(2n+1)k=2n\ell$. Since consecutive integers are coprime, the
solutions with $k,\ell\ge1$ are precisely
$k=2nr$, $\ell=(2n+1)r$, $r\ge1$. Thus the inner product in (17) is
a sum of phase-weighted terms
$v_{2nr}\overline{v_{(2n+1)r}}$. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its modulus
by

$$
\left(\sum_{r\ge1}|v_{2nr}|^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{r\ge1}|v_{(2n+1)r}|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le T_{2n}(v).
$$

Both $V_m$ preserve the norm, so expanding the square gives (18).
The tail $T_{2n}(v)$ tends to zero by square summability. The defect
terms therefore tend to the positive constant $2\|v\|^2$ when
$v\ne0$, which proves divergence. The same calculation shows
$V_{2n+1}^{*}V_{2n}\to0$ strongly: its squared norm on $v$ is
$\sum_{r\ge1}|v_{(2n+1)r}|^2\le T_{2n+1}(v)$.

A diagonal reweighting of the counting basis does not rescue the raw
isometric shifts. If $\|e_k\|^2=w_k>0$ and every raw prime map
$e_k\mapsto e_{pk}$ is an isometry, then $w_{pk}=w_k$ for all primes
$p$ and integers $k$. Unique factorization gives $w_k=w_1$ for every
$k$. Phase gauges already fall under (16).

This written Hilbert-space calculation rules out applying the
finite-defect hypothesis of the unitary theorem above to the natural
counting representation, even after support-preserving prime phases.
It does not rule out a different representation or a renormalized
signed readout, and it gives no bound on $E(x)$ or zeta zeros. Such a
new representation would have to retain the boundary at one and
identify its spectral form with the actual prime-power explicit
formula rather than impose the critical line through unitarity.
