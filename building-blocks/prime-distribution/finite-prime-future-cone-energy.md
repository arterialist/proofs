# Completed prime future cones increase quadratic energy

Let $S$ be a nonempty finite set of primes, $Q_S=\prod_{q\in S}q$, and

$$
F_S(m)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid Q_S\\d\le m}}\mu(d),
\qquad E(S)=\sum_{m\ge1}F_S(m)^2.
$$

The divisor sum is zero once $m\ge Q_S$, so $E(S)$ is finite and positive.
This is the complete additive-time history of the signed multiplicative
divisor cube generated by $S$.

**Energy theorem.** For any prime $p\notin S$,

$$
F_{S\cup\{p\}}(m)=F_S(m)-F_S(\lfloor m/p\rfloor),
\qquad
E(S\cup\{p\})\ge(\sqrt p-1)^2E(S).\tag{1}
$$

Indeed, the first identity pairs each divisor $d\mid Q_S$ with $pd$,
whose Möbius coefficient is $-\mu(d)$. Put
$D_pF(m)=F(\lfloor m/p\rfloor)$. Every value of $F_S$ occurs exactly
$p$ times in $D_pF_S$ at nonnegative indices, so
$\|D_pF_S\|_2=\sqrt p\|F_S\|_2$. The reverse triangle inequality proves
(1). Hence every added prime $p\ge5$ **strictly increases** this completed
quadratic energy.

For the actual first three primes the energies are
$E(\{2\})=1$, $E(\{2,3\})=4$, and $E(\{2,3,5\})=26$. In the last
step $\langle F_{\{2,3\}},D_5F_{\{2,3\}}\rangle=-1$, so the signed
cross term amplifies the energy. These values follow by listing the
finite prefixes; they are not asymptotic claims.

The observation horizon also grows from $Q_S$ to $pQ_S$. After dividing
by that horizon, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\left|\frac{E(S\cup\{p\})}{pQ_S}-\frac{E(S)}{Q_S}\right|
\le\left(\frac1p+\frac2{\sqrt p}\right)\frac{E(S)}{Q_S}.
$$

Thus the *energy density* approaches its previous value as a new prime
$p\to\infty$ with $S$ fixed. The theorem concerns unnormalized energy;
it does not exclude every possible normalized invariant.

The finite cone has an exact zero-total-mass identity:
$\sum_{d\mid Q_S}\mu(d)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-1)=0$. Its higher
multiplicative moments are
$\sum_{d\mid Q_S}\mu(d)d^k=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^k)$ for $k>0$;
their magnitudes usually grow when another prime is added. Thus a zero total charge is not an energy
conservation law.

For the Abel energy $Z_N$ defined in
[the smooth-transport boundary note](../dynamics-and-feedback/log-squared-smooth-transport-boundary.md),
each fixed finite $S$ also satisfies

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}N Z_N(F_S)=2E(S).
$$

Consequently adjoining a fixed $p\ge5$ eventually increases the same
Abel statistic. This does not decide the critical moving-cutoff problem:
there $S$ grows with $N$ and most of the completed cone lies beyond the
observation horizon $N$. The theorem rules out a proposed *global*
quadratic conservation principle. A useful damping law would have to
control the actual truncated signed correlations.
