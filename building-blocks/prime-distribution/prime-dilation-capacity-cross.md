# Prime-dilation face cross in the hard capacity weight

Let $D,N\in\mathbb N$, let $p$ be prime, and retain the two literal
faces from the audited dilation identity

\[
 U_p=\sum_{\substack{\max(D,N/p)<m\le N\\p\nmid m}}\mu(m),
 \qquad
 L_p=\sum_{\substack{D/p<m\le\min(D,N/p)\\p\nmid m}}\mu(m).
\]

No endpoint has been rounded or extended.  The exact identity is

\[
 B_{D,N}=U_p-L_p,
 \qquad B_{D,N}=\sum_{D<d\le N}\mu(d).                 \tag{1}
\]

Thus the signed cross generated by squaring the two-face formula is

\[
 B_{D,N}^{2}=U_p^2+L_p^2-2U_pL_p.                  \tag{2}
\]

For the capacity in (14a), put

\[
 \ell_N(B)=\log\frac{eN}{|B|+N^{-1}},\qquad
 \Psi_N(B)=\frac{((|B|-N^{-1})_+)^2}{\ell_N(B)}.
\]

Since $B_{D,N}$ is an integer, either $B_{D,N}=0$, when the capacity is
zero, or 

\[
 \boxed{
 \Psi_N(B_{D,N})=
 \frac{U_p^2+L_p^2-2U_pL_p-2|B_{D,N}|/N+N^{-2}}
      {\ell_N(B_{D,N})}.}                              \tag{3}
\]

The denominator and the threshold correction in (3) are independent of
$p$.  Consequently, for every finite prime band \(\mathcal P\), including
\(p\asymp N/D\),

\[
 |\mathcal P|\Psi_N(B_{D,N})=
 \frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}(U_p^2+L_p^2-2U_pL_p)
 -|\mathcal P|(2|B_{D,N}|/N-N^{-2})}
 {\ell_N(B_{D,N})}.                                    \tag{4}
\]

Hence a proposed bound \(\Psi_N(B_{D,N})\le R\) obtained from this band is
equivalent to the signed correlation requirement

\[
 \boxed{
 2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}U_pL_p\ge
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}(U_p^2+L_p^2)
 -|\mathcal P|\left(R\ell_N(B_{D,N})
   +\frac{2|B_{D,N}|}{N}-\frac1{N^2}\right).}          \tag{5}
\]

This is the exact capacity-weighted target.  It requires positive correlation
between the two signed Möbius faces.  Their opposite signs in (1) do not imply
that correlation.

There is an actual near-transition counterexample to any pointwise favorable
cross assertion.  Take

\[
 N=146,\qquad D=13,\qquad p=11,qquad
 \frac{p}{N/D}=\frac{143}{146}.
\]

The moving endpoints and coprimality restriction give

\[
 U_{11}=\sum_{\substack{14\le m\le146\\11\nmid m}}\mu(m)=1,
 \qquad
 L_{11}=\sum_{\substack{2\le m\le13\\11\nmid m}}\mu(m)=-3.
\]

Indeed $M(13)=-3$, $M(146)=1$, so $B_{13,146}=4$.  The lower sum is
the exact finite list

\[
 \mu(2)+\mu(3)+\mu(4)+\mu(5)+\mu(6)+\mu(7)+\mu(8)
 +\mu(9)+\mu(10)+\mu(12)+\mu(13)=-3.
\]

Equation (1) then forces $U_{11}=4+(-3)=1$.  Therefore

\[
 -2U_{11}L_{11}=6>0,                                  \tag{6}
\]

so the signed cross increases the square at a prime within about two percent
of (N/D).  In the literal capacity weight its contribution is

\[
 \frac{6}{\log(e\,146/(4+1/146))}>0.
\]

Thus no uniform pointwise negative-cross theorem holds even in the intended
transition range.  A valid continuation must prove the averaged correlation
(5), or a stronger weighted variant, using arithmetic information beyond the
endpoint geometry.  This reduction asserts no infinite affine bulk bound.
