# The actual exterior ground-state process and centered Green covariance

This is a written proof for the actual full theta jump form. No theorem in this chapter is formalized in Lean. The killed-ground-state and constrained-domain inputs are established in the [ground-pole theorem](theta-killed-return-ground-pole.md) and [constrained Schur construction](theta-constrained-schur-across-ground-pole.md).

Let $D=L_O$ be the full killed theta operator outside a fixed finite core $C=[-R,R]$, $R>0$. The source [theta-killed-return-ground-pole.md](theta-killed-return-ground-pole.md) proves that its lowest eigenvalue $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$ is simple, with a normalized eigenfunction $\psi>0$ almost everywhere, and a gap
$$
D\psi=\alpha\psi,\qquad\|\psi\|_{L^2(O,\nu)}=1,
\qquad d=\inf(\sigma(D)\setminus\{\alpha\})-\alpha>0.
\tag{1}
$$
The construction below assumes no smoothness of $\psi$, no positive lower bound for it, and no completeness assertion for a prescribed family of theta modes. It retains the actual killed form and its full continuous and prime-power jump measure.

## 1. A conservative symmetric Markov semigroup

Define a probability measure and a unitary map by
$$
d\mu(u)=\psi(u)^2d\nu(u),\qquad
V_\psi:L^2(O,\mu)\to L^2(O,\nu),\qquad V_\psi F=\psi F.
\tag{2}
$$
The inverse is division by $\psi$ almost everywhere. It is an isometry on these weighted spaces, regardless of whether division is bounded in an unweighted space.

Set
$$
\mathsf P_tF=e^{\alpha t}\psi^{-1}e^{-tD}(\psi F),\qquad
\mathsf A=V_\psi^{-1}(D-\alpha)V_\psi.
\tag{3}
$$
This is a strongly continuous symmetric contraction semigroup on $L^2(\mu)$, generated by the nonnegative self-adjoint $\mathsf A$. Positivity follows from that of the actual killed semigroup. The ground-state equation gives the exact conservation law
$$
\mathsf P_t1=1.
\tag{4}
$$
If $0\le F\le1$, then $0\le\psi F\le\psi$, so positivity in (3) gives $0\le\mathsf P_tF\le1$. Thus it is a genuine conservative Markov semigroup, not only a unitary similarity of a quadratic form. Symmetry and (4) imply stationarity of $\mu$.

The exact operator and form domains are
$$
D(\mathsf A)=\{F:\psi F\in D(D)\},\qquad
\mathsf V=\{F:\psi F\in D(E_O)\},
$$
$$
\mathsf E(F)=E_O(\psi F)-\alpha\|\psi F\|_\nu^2.
\tag{5}
$$
Constants are the entire zero eigenspace, and
$$
\mathsf E(F)\ge d\|F-\mu F\|_\mu^2,
\qquad
\|\mathsf P_tF\|_\mu\le e^{-dt}\|F\|_\mu\quad(\mu F=0).
\tag{6}
$$
The centering is with respect to $\mu$, not $\nu$.

## 2. The full transformed conductance and cancellation of killing

Write $b(u)=2\cosh(u/2)$, $d\nu=b\Phi\,du$, and let $J_O(du,dv)$ be the directed symmetric internal conductance of the original killed form. Its continuous part is
$$
1_O(u)1_O(v)\Phi(u)\Phi(v)r(|u-v|)\,du\,dv,
\qquad r(a)=\frac{e^{-a/2}}{1-e^{-2a}}.
$$
For every $q\ge2$, it also includes both directed atoms at $v=u\pm\log q$, with weight
$$
\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\sqrt q}\,
1_O(u)1_O(u\pm\log q)\Phi(u)\Phi(u\pm\log q)\,du.
$$
The factor $1/2$ in the form below compensates for these two orientations. With the original killing rate $k_C(u)$,
$$
E_O(h)=\tfrac12\iint|h(v)-h(u)|^2J_O(du,dv)
             +\int_O k_C(u)|h(u)|^2d\nu(u).
\tag{7}
$$
This is the maximal killed jump-form domain already established in the source construction; the killing can be unbounded near the hard boundary.

Define
$$
J_\psi(du,dv)=\psi(u)\psi(v)J_O(du,dv).
\tag{8}
$$
Then the exact transformed form and its maximal domain are
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathsf E(F)=\tfrac12\iint|F(v)-F(u)|^2J_\psi(du,dv),
\quad
\mathsf V=\left\{F\in L^2(\mu):\iint|\Delta F|^2J_\psi<\infty\right\}.
\quad}
\tag{9}
$$
There is no remaining killing term. Its disappearance follows from the weak ground-state equation, not a pointwise differentiation of $\psi$.

Here are the domain details. First suppose $F\in\mathsf V$ is bounded, $|F|\le M$. Put $h=\psi F$. The function $\psi|F|^2=|h|^2/\psi$ lies in $D(E_O)$. Indeed the perspective map
$(a,z)\mapsto|z|^2/a$, extended by zero at $(0,0)$, is Lipschitz on the convex cone $a\ge0,|z|\le Ma$, with the bound
$$
|\Delta(\psi|F|^2)|\le M^2|\Delta\psi|+2M|\Delta h|.
$$
Its norm and killing integrals are bounded by those of $M^2\psi$. The maximal original jump domain thus justifies this weak test. Pointwise edge algebra gives
$$
E_O(\psi F)-\operatorname{Re}E_O(\psi,\psi|F|^2)
=\tfrac12\iint\psi(u)\psi(v)|\Delta F|^2J_O.
$$
The weak eigen-equation evaluates the subtracted term as $\alpha\int|F|^2d\mu$. The killing terms cancel exactly, proving (9) for these bounded domain vectors.

Conversely, suppose $F$ is bounded and its right side in (9) is finite. The elementary identity
$$
\psi(v)F(v)-\psi(u)F(u)
=\sqrt{\psi(u)\psi(v)}\,[F(v)-F(u)]
 +(\sqrt{\psi(v)}-\sqrt{\psi(u)})
       [\sqrt{\psi(v)}F(v)+\sqrt{\psi(u)}F(u)]
$$
implies
$$
|\Delta(\psi F)|^2
\le2\psi(u)\psi(v)|\Delta F|^2+2M^2|\Delta\psi|^2.
\tag{10}
$$
The original killing term is at most $M^2\int k_C\psi^2d\nu$. Thus $\psi F$ belongs to the original maximal form domain, proving the converse without a bound on $\psi^{-1}$.

For arbitrary real $F$, truncate its values to $[-N,N]$. The Markov form in (5) makes these truncations admissible with no larger energy whenever $F\in\mathsf V$; Fatou then gives the jump energy at most $\mathsf E(F)$. In the reverse direction, if the jump energy is finite, the bounded converse puts all truncations in $\mathsf V$, with bounded energies converging to that jump energy by dominated convergence. Closedness and $L^2$ convergence give membership and the reverse inequality. Apply the real argument separately to real and imaginary parts. This proves the full assertion (9).

Relative to the new invariant measure, the actual transition rate is therefore
$$
q^\psi(u,dv)=\frac{\psi(v)}{\psi(u)}q(u,dv),\qquad u,v\in O,
\tag{11}
$$
where $q$ is the original internal rate. In particular (11) retains every atom at $\pm\log q$ and the entire continuous density. It is a measurable rate identity almost everywhere, not an assertion that the rates are bounded.

The continuous rate still has infinite activity almost everywhere. At an interior Lebesgue point with $\psi(u)>0$, the set where nearby $\psi(v)\ge\psi(u)/2$ has density one. Each sufficiently small dyadic annulus contains a fixed positive fraction of such points. Since $r(|u-v|)\asymp1/|u-v|$, its integral over these annuli diverges. Local integrability of $\psi$ follows from $\psi\in L^2(\nu)$ and the positive smooth density of $\nu$. Thus (4) proves conservativity despite the infinite small-jump rate; there is no finite-rate holding-time approximation hidden in it.

## 3. Centered Green–Kubo covariance

Let $f,q\in L^2(O,\nu)$ be orthogonal to $\psi$, and put
$$
F=f/\psi,\qquad Q=q/\psi,
\qquad G=(D-\alpha)^{-1}\big|_{\psi^\perp}.
$$
Then $F,Q\in L^2(\mu)$ are centered. The centered spectral gap gives absolute convergence of
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal C(f,q):=\langle Gf,q\rangle_\nu
 =\int_0^\infty\langle\mathsf P_tF,Q\rangle_\mu\,dt.
\quad}
\tag{12}
$$
Indeed the integrand has absolute value at most $e^{-dt}\|f\|_\nu\|q\|_\nu$. Formula (12) is the centered Green–Kubo integral of the genuine stationary process in (3), not the divergent uncentered return clock at $z=\alpha$.

The probabilistic covariance can equivalently be understood through the Markov transition operators on the standard Borel exterior space. Their stationary two-time law gives
$\langle\mathsf P_tF,Q\rangle_\mu=\mathbb E_\mu[F(X_t)\overline{Q(X_0)}]$.
No Feller or boundary-path regularity is assumed. In a stationary realization, the $L^2$ time integrals are legitimate by strong continuity, and reversibility gives the precise normalization
$$
\frac1T\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
 \left(\int_0^T F(X_s)ds\right)
 \overline{\left(\int_0^T Q(X_s)ds\right)}\right]
=2\int_0^T(1-t/T)\langle\mathsf P_tF,Q\rangle_\mu\,dt
\longrightarrow2\mathcal C(f,q).
\tag{13}
$$
Thus our $\mathcal C$ is half the asymptotic covariance per unit time. No central limit theorem or Gaussian law is needed.

If $q\ne0$, positivity and injectivity of $G$ on $\psi^\perp$ give $\mathcal C(q,q)>0$. The exact one-direction Schur covariance is
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal C(f,f)-\frac{|\mathcal C(q,f)|^2}{\mathcal C(q,q)}
=\min_{c\in\mathbb C}\mathcal C(f-cq,f-cq)\ge0.
\quad}
\tag{14}
$$
The coefficient is $c=\mathcal C(f,q)/\mathcal C(q,q)$ with the inner product linear in the first slot. This is optimal linear regression in the Green covariance, or the corresponding constrained quadratic form. It is not an assertion that a non-Gaussian conditional expectation has the same variance. For $q=0$, simply omit the subtraction.

## 4. A full-edge variational criterion for source and crossing bounds

Give the directed transformed edge space the measure $\tfrac12J_\psi$, restricted to antisymmetric fields, and let $\partial F(u,v)=F(v)-F(u)$. It is a closed gradient with domain (9), and $\mathsf A=\partial^*\partial$. The adjoint is weak divergence; no separation into potentially infinite arrival and departure integrals is needed.

For centered $F=f/\psi$, let $U=\mathsf A^{-1}F$. Then
$U\in D(\mathsf A)$, $j_f=\partial U\in D(\partial^*)$, and $\partial^*j_f=F$. The exact Dirichlet and flow variational identities are
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal C(f,f)
=\sup_{V\in\mathsf V}\{2\operatorname{Re}\langle F,V\rangle_\mu-\mathsf E(V)\}
=\min_{\substack{j\in D(\partial^*)\\\partial^*j=F}}\|j\|_{L^2(J_\psi/2)}^2.
\quad}
\tag{15}
$$
The first identity follows by completing the energy square about $U$; constants do not affect it because $F$ is centered. For the second, every feasible flow is $j_f+k$, where $\partial^*k=0$, and
$\langle j_f,k\rangle=\langle U,\partial^*k\rangle=0$. Hence its squared norm is $\|j_f\|^2+\|k\|^2$.

This yields a source-specific upper bound beyond use of the gap: any explicitly constructed full-conductance flow whose weak divergence is the actual centered source gives
$\mathcal C(f,f)\le\|j\|^2$. Conversely a comparison cannot substitute a flow on an incomplete set of arithmetic edges without checking its divergence and cost in the measure (8).

For the subtraction in (14), the precise conditional versions are
$$
\begin{split}
\mathcal C(f,f)-\frac{|\mathcal C(q,f)|^2}{\mathcal C(q,q)}
&=\sup_{\substack{V\in\mathsf V\\\langle V,Q\rangle_\mu=0}}
       \{2\operatorname{Re}\langle F,V\rangle_\mu-\mathsf E(V)\}\\
&=\min_{\substack{c\in\mathbb C,\ j\in D(\partial^*)\\
                   \partial^*j=F-cQ}}\|j\|^2.
\end{split}
\tag{16}
$$
The maximizing potential is $\mathsf A^{-1}(F-cQ)$ with the coefficient from (14); its pairing with $Q$ vanishes exactly. Thus the conditional Green covariance and its current interpretation retain the same source constraint, rather than merely applying an abstract positivity bound.

Equations (9), (12), and (15)–(16) use the complete exterior jump process. The ground-state transform has removed killing by the actual eigen-equation and centers the stationary channel before time integration. It is therefore distinct from extending the raw positive-return Laplace integral through its pole. None of these covariance identities proves a core Schur comparison or the theta sharp gap.
